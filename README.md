# Singularity

## Foundations

- **[The Coming Technological Singularity: How to Survive in the Post-Human Era](https://edoras.sdsu.edu/~vinge/misc/singularity.html)** — Vernor Vinge (1993). Argues that creating superhuman intelligence could trigger runaway technological change beyond our ability to predict, and explores possible routes to human survival.
- **[The Law of Accelerating Returns](https://www.writingsbyraykurzweil.com/the-law-of-accelerating-returns)** — Ray Kurzweil (2001). Argues that technological progress accelerates through successive paradigms, leading toward a merger of biological and machine intelligence.

## Forecasts and scenarios

- **[The Most Important Century](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/)** — Holden Karnofsky (2021). An essay series arguing that AI-driven scientific progress could make this century decisive for a civilization whose trajectory persists for billions of years.
- **[Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead](https://situational-awareness.ai/)** — Leopold Aschenbrenner (2024). An essay series forecasting near-term AGI and an intelligence explosion, arguing for massive infrastructure investment, stronger lab security, alignment research, and democratic leadership.
- **[AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/)** — Daniel Kokotajlo et al. (2025). A scenario informed by forecasts in which AI automates research and rapidly reaches superintelligence, exploring how racing or slowing down could lead to different outcomes.
- **[Europe 2031](https://europe2031.ai/)** — Daan Juijn et al. (2026). A scenario of European decline through dependence on American and Chinese AI, advocating compute investment, industrial strengths, and alliances that give Europe bargaining power.

## Abundance and acceleration

- **[The Techno-Optimist Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/)** — Marc Andreessen (2023). Presents technological acceleration, competitive markets, intelligence, and energy as the foundations of growth and abundance.
- **[Why AI Will Save the World](https://a16z.com/ai-will-save-the-world/)** — Marc Andreessen (2023). Advocates rapid AI development, competition, and open source, arguing that widespread intelligence and stronger defenses offer greater benefits than restricting development.
- **[Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)** — Dario Amodei (2024). Sketches how powerful AI could accelerate medicine, reduce poverty, and strengthen democracy if its risks are managed.
- **[The Intelligence Age](https://ia.samaltman.com/)** — Sam Altman (2024). Argues that scaling deep learning, expanding computing infrastructure, and making intelligence widely available could produce extraordinary shared prosperity.
- **[Personal Superintelligence](https://www.meta.com/superintelligence/)** — Mark Zuckerberg (2025). Envisions personal AI that helps individuals pursue their goals, create, and connect, with broad access to superintelligence serving human agency.
- **[The Gentle Singularity](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity)** — Sam Altman (2025). Argues that the transition to superintelligence has begun and could bring abundant intelligence and energy while much of everyday human life remains recognizable.
- **[The Future is for Everyone](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/08/the-future-is-for-everyone/)** — Meta (2026). Proposes broadly distributed superintelligence for individual empowerment and invention, arguing that competing people and institutions can provide checks on concentrated power.

## Safety and governance

- **[Pausing AI Developments Isn't Enough. We Need to Shut It All Down](https://intelligence.org/2023/04/07/pausing-ai-developments-isnt-enough-we-need-to-shut-it-all-down/)** — Eliezer Yudkowsky (2023). Argues that building superhuman AI under current conditions would likely cause human extinction, and calls for an indefinite, internationally enforced moratorium on large training runs.
- **[A Narrow Path](https://www.narrowpath.co/)** — Andrea Miotti et al. (2024). Proposes preventing superintelligence for twenty years, establishing international oversight, and building the scientific foundations for transformative AI that remains under human control.
- **[Keep the Future Human](https://keepthefuturehuman.ai/)** — Anthony Aguirre (2025). Advocates powerful, trustworthy AI tools while restricting autonomous general intelligence through compute oversight, compute caps, developer liability, and safety standards.
- **[Towards Humanist Superintelligence](https://microsoft.ai/news/towards-humanist-superintelligence/)** — Mustafa Suleyman (2025). Advocates bounded, controllable AI focused on concrete problems, with human agency and welfare guiding the development of superhuman capabilities.
- **[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/)** — Thomas Larsen et al., AI Futures Project (2026). A policy proposal presented as a scenario: delay superintelligence through an international agreement, make AI research public, and spread frontier capabilities across countries.
- **[The Adolescence of Technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology)** — Dario Amodei (2026). Argues that realizing powerful AI's benefits requires confronting loss of control, destructive misuse, authoritarianism, economic disruption, and destabilizing technological change through proportionate safeguards.

## Power, distribution, and counterarguments

- **[Moore's Law for Everything](https://moores.samaltman.com/)** — Sam Altman (2021). Anticipates AI shifting economic power from labor to capital and proposes taxing companies and land to distribute the resulting wealth.
- **[The Ambition Singularity](https://danfaggella.com/flex/)** — Daniel Faggella (2022). Argues that the pursuit of status and power will draw the world's most ambitious people toward creating, controlling, or regulating AGI.
- **[AGI and the EMH: Markets are not expecting aligned or unaligned AI in the next 30 years](https://agiandtheemh.com/)** — Trevor Chow, Basil Halperin, and J. Zachary Mazlish (2023). Applies the efficient-market hypothesis to argue that near-term transformative AI should raise real interest rates, interpreting low rates as evidence for longer timelines or market mispricing.
- **[My Techno-Optimism](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/techno_optimism.html)** — Vitalik Buterin (2023). Advocates accelerating technologies that favor defense, decentralization, and democracy, while exercising particular caution around superintelligent AI and preserving human agency.
- **[AI as Normal Technology](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology)** — Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor (2025). Argues that AI's impact will be shaped by gradual adoption and institutions, and advocates democratic governance and resilience rather than treating superintelligence as the default frame.
- **[Artificial Power: 2025 Landscape Report](https://ainowinstitute.org/publications/research/ai-now-2025-landscape-report)** — Kate Brennan, Amba Kak, and Sarah Myers West, AI Now Institute (2025). Argues that widespread AI deployment concentrates corporate power, and proposes worker organizing, public-interest regulation, and democratic institutions as ways to reclaim control over its direction.
- **[Gradual Disempowerment](https://gradual-disempowerment.ai/)** — Jan Kulveit et al. (2025). Argues that incremental automation can erode human influence over economies, culture, and governments through competitive pressures, even without a sudden takeover or coordinated AI power-seeking.
- **[The Intelligence Curse](https://intelligence-curse.ai/)** — Luke Drago and Rudolf Laine (2025). Argues that replacing human labor weakens powerful institutions' incentives to serve people, and advocates broadly distributed AI, human augmentation, and stronger democratic institutions.
- **[The Commanding Heights](https://commanding-heights.com/)** — Will Manidis and Joe Roberts (2026). Argues that as AI models become commodities, durable wealth and power will accrue to those controlling the scarce assets and institutions through which intelligence reaches the wider economy.
